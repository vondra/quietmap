title: South America
intro: Traffic counts in four countries; on the 2026 map, estimates from the national road register in four and class defaults in four (global road model elsewhere on the 2024 map). The only train timetable is the Buenos Aires Subte's 2019 archive (2026 map only).

<!-- SUBPAGES -->

Argentina, Chile, Colombia and Peru publish traffic counts. Colombia also counts trucks and buses per section; the other three publish daily totals and their vehicle classes are estimated. On the 2026 map, counts reach 66 % of the motorway, trunk and primary length in Colombia, 62 % in Argentina, 6 % in Peru and 3 % in Chile; Chile's counts date from 2024 and 2025 (2022 to 2024 on the 2024 map) and Argentina's from 2017–18.

On the 2026 map, Bolivia, Ecuador, Paraguay and Venezuela (and uncounted network roads in Argentina, Chile, Colombia and Peru) estimate main-road volumes from national road-register attributes (surface, road type, administration), doubled inside manually drawn boxes around the largest cities and multiplied by 1.4 in a second list of cities, with regional vehicle-mix tables on the country pages; Brazil, Uruguay, Guyana and Suriname use class defaults, while unmatched main roads in Chile and Colombia (10 % and 21 % of their length) follow the building-demand model. Several government servers are unreachable from abroad, so some layers come from community mirrors.

On the 2024 map, road-register tables and manually drawn city boxes are not used: uncounted motorways, trunk and primary roads follow the model of trips within 1, 5 and 15 km in Argentina, Chile, Colombia and Peru and class defaults by road class and lanes in the other eight countries; secondary and tertiary roads follow the trips made within 5 km, and streets the trips mapped buildings make ([global road model](/about/data-preparation)).

Where vehicle classes are not counted (and outside the 2026 regional mix tables above), a main road takes the medium and heavy shares of counted roads of its class, a street 1 % medium and 2 % heavy, and motorcycles come from national fleets (WHO 2023): 47 % of a town street's traffic in Colombia, 16 % in Argentina, 7.7 % in Brazil.

Railways: on the 2026 map, the only timetable is the Buenos Aires Subte's 2019 archive (one busy Wednesday; only the Premetro runs on the surface). On the 2024 map, that archive does not overlap 2024 and supplies no scheduled train counts. All other lines (and all lines on the 2024 map) use the [world railway defaults](/about/data-preparation) (80 passenger and 20 freight trains per day on main lines). Surface metro sections are included; see the [railway method](/about/data-preparation).

Industry: Chile, Bolivia, Brazil and Venezuela have national power plant registers; Chile adds tailings dams and Peru mine outlines. Elsewhere power plants come from Global Energy Monitor.

Ships: Global Fishing Watch AIS vessel density.

Layer construction is described on the [data preparation page](/about/data-preparation).
