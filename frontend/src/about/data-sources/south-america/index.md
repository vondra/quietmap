<!-- SUBPAGES -->

Argentina, Chile, Colombia and Peru publish traffic counts. They reach 66 % of the motorway, trunk and primary length in Colombia, 62 % in Argentina, 6 % in Peru and 3 % in Chile; the rest is estimated. Colombia also counts trucks and buses per section. Chile's counts date from 2024 and 2025, Argentina's from 2017-18.

Bolivia, Ecuador, Paraguay and Venezuela publish their road network without traffic. Volumes there are estimated from register attributes: surface, road type, state or provincial status. The tables are on the country pages. Several government servers are unreachable from abroad, so some layers come from community mirrors. Brazil, Uruguay, Guyana and Suriname use class defaults.

In counted and estimated countries alike, estimates (never counts) are doubled inside manually drawn boxes around the largest cities and multiplied by 1.4 in a second list of cities. Counted totals in Argentina, Chile and Peru and the register estimates of Bolivia, Ecuador, Paraguay and Venezuela keep a vehicle mix estimated by region (listed on the country pages). On other uncounted roads a main road takes the medium and heavy shares of counted roads of its class, a street 1 % medium and 2 % heavy, and motorcycles come from national fleets (WHO 2023): 47 % of a town street's traffic in Colombia, 16 % in Argentina, 7.7 % in Brazil. Uncounted streets carry the trips their buildings make, uncounted secondary and tertiary roads the trips made within 5 km of them; in Chile and Colombia main roads matched to neither count nor register (10 % and 21 % of their length) follow one model of the trips made around them.

Railways: the only timetable is the Buenos Aires Subte's 2019 archive, whose Premetro runs on the surface. A main line defaults to 80 passenger and 20 freight trains per day. Surface metro sections are included; see the [railway method](/about/data-preparation).

Industry: Chile, Bolivia, Brazil and Venezuela have national power plant registers; Chile adds tailings dams and Peru mine outlines. Elsewhere power plants come from Global Energy Monitor.

Ships: Global Fishing Watch AIS vessel density.

Layer construction is described on the [data preparation page](/about/data-preparation).
