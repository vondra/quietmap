title: South America
intro: Traffic counts in four countries and the global road model elsewhere. No railway timetable covers the 2024 or 2026 map windows.
---

<!-- SUBPAGES -->

Argentina, Chile, Colombia and Peru publish traffic counts. Colombia also counts trucks and buses per section; the other three publish daily totals and their vehicle classes are estimated. On the 2026 map, counts reach 66 % of the motorway, trunk and primary length in Colombia, 62 % in Argentina, 6 % in Peru and 3 % in Chile; Chile's counts date from 2024 and 2025 (2022 and 2023 on the 2024 map) and Argentina's from 2018.

All other countries — Bolivia, Brazil, Ecuador, Guyana, Paraguay, Suriname, Uruguay and Venezuela — and every uncounted road in the region follow the [global road model](/about/data-preparation): road class and lanes on main roads, trips made within 5 km on secondary and tertiary roads, and the trips mapped buildings make on streets. The model does not assign traffic from road-register attributes or manually drawn city boxes.

Where vehicle classes are not counted, a main road takes the medium and heavy shares of counted roads of its class, a street 1 % medium and 2 % heavy, and motorcycles come from national fleets (WHO 2023): 47 % of a town street's traffic in Colombia, 16 % in Argentina, 7.7 % in Brazil.

Railways: the 2019 archive of the Buenos Aires Subte does not overlap the 2024 or 2026 observation windows, so it supplies no scheduled train counts; all lines use the [world railway defaults](/about/data-preparation) (80 passenger and 20 freight trains per day on main lines). Surface metro sections are included; see the [railway method](/about/data-preparation).

Industry: Chile, Bolivia, Brazil and Venezuela have national power plant registers; Chile adds tailings dams and Peru mine outlines. Elsewhere power plants come from Global Energy Monitor.

Ships: Global Fishing Watch AIS vessel density.

Layer construction is described on the [data preparation page](/about/data-preparation).
