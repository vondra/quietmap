## Roads

Traffic volumes: no national counts. Nigeria-specific estimates by OpenStreetMap class, scaled ×2.5 in Lagos (motorway 87,500), ×2 in Kano, Abuja and Ibadan, and ×1.4 in 20 other cities, among them Port Harcourt, Benin City, Kaduna and Maiduguri.

| Road class | Open country | Kano, Abuja, Ibadan (×2) | Other listed cities (×1.4) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Motorway | 35,000 | 70,000 | 49,000 |
| Trunk | 12,000 | 24,000 | 16,800 |
| Primary | 6,000 | 12,000 | 8,400 |
| Secondary | 3,000 | 6,000 | 4,200 |
| Tertiary | 1,500 | 3,000 | 2,100 |
| Residential | 700 | 1,400 | 980 |

Vehicles per day, both directions. Slip roads take half the value of their road. Service roads, tracks and unclassified roads keep the world default.

Vehicle mix: estimated by location. The freight row applies only to motorways and trunk roads around Apapa and Tin Can ports and along the Lagos to Ibadan expressway; other Lagos streets keep the city mix.

| Where | Cars and vans | Medium | Heavy | Motorcycles |

| Line | Passenger trains per day | Freight trains per day |

| Light rail and tram in Lagos | 250 | 0 |
| Light rail elsewhere (Abuja) | 60 | 0 |
| Industrial track | 0 | 6 |

| Abuja to Kaduna | 8 | 6 |
| Itakpe to Warri | 2 | 20 |

The 250 passenger trains on main-line track inside Lagos are sized for the commuter corridor and overstate every other track there.
